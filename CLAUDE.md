## Merge gotchas

When joining `occupancy_panel` (which carries `zone_id`) onto `segments` (which also carries
`zone_id`), drop `zone_id` and `capacity` from the panel **before** the merge to avoid
`zone_id_x` / `zone_id_y` splits. Pattern used in `revenue/forecast.py` and `scenarios/runner.py`:

```python
panel = occupancy_panel.drop(
    columns=[c for c in ("zone_id", "capacity") if c in occupancy_panel.columns]
).merge(seg, on="segment_id", how="inner")
```

## Synthetic baseline calibration

`demand/synthetic_baseline.py` scales each segment's weekly profile between its own off-peak
floor and peak, interpolated by a latent demand intensity. Every anchor is absolute and lives
in `config/demand_baseline.yaml`: covariates are standardised as (log1p(x) - center) / scale
with fixed centers and scales, intensity maps to peak occupancy between fixed
`intensity_low` / `intensity_high`, and the land-use mix is centred on the same fixed centers.
A segment's baseline therefore never depends on which other segments are loaded (audit
finding 24); never reintroduce quantiles, z-scores or means taken over the loaded set.

Centers, scales and intensity bounds come in **anchor sets**, one per reference set of
segments: `synthetic_fixture` (the demo fixture's moments, rounded) and `philadelphia`
(the priced curb of the real `segments.parquet`, generated by
`scripts/derive_baseline_anchors.py` between BEGIN/END markers; `--check` exits 1 when stale,
so rerun it after script 02). `baseline_occupancy` takes the set from
`segments.attrs["segment_source"]["kind"]` via `source_anchor_sets` (fixture ->
`synthetic_fixture`, file or Parking Authority inventory -> `philadelphia`) and raises when
the segments carry no source: `pd.concat` and similar drop `attrs`, so pass `anchor_set=`
then. The weights and the occupancy levels (`peak_target`, `low_demand_peak`, off-peak floor)
are shared and are stated, not measured; the anchors only place a segment relative to its
reference set. `baseline_occupancy` warns when more than `max_clipped_share` of segments sit at
the cap or floor; `panel.attrs["calibration"]` has the set and the shares. Segments the
Parking Authority panel covers should use observed occupancy instead.

## Arnott-Inci zone pricing

`_arnott_zone_prices()` in `scenarios/runner.py` must guard against zones present in the
occupancy panel but absent from `current_prices`. Pattern:

```python
cur_row = current_prices[current_prices["zone_id"] == zone]
if cur_row.empty:
    continue
```

**Peak statistic: the zone's busiest priced hour of capacity-weighted occupancy.** For each
zone, average occupancy across its segments weighted by capacity, hour by hour, over the
zone's priced hours only; take the maximum; price so that hour sits at
`1 - target_vacancy`. Why this statistic and not another:

- Arnott-Inci treat a downtown area as one pool that a cruiser searches block by block, so
  search depends on the zone aggregate, not on any one segment. Hence capacity-weighted
  zone-hours, not segment-hours.
- A zone has one rate across all its priced hours, so the only rate that holds the aggregate
  at or below the target in *every* priced hour is the one that brings the busiest one there.
  Hence the maximum, which is also what "eliminates cruising at the busiest hour" says.
- Unpriced hours are excluded because the rate cannot act on them. Pooling them in (the old
  all-hours q90) let overnight residential occupancy set a downtown rate.
- The panel is a typical-week profile, each hour already an average over weeks, so its
  maximum is not one noisy draw. On real week-by-week data, revisit: a high quantile of
  zone-hours would be more stable.

The choice moves the full-PBD row's sign (audit 2026-10-14, finding 3), so do not swap it
without rerunning scripts 05/06 and updating the prose. Segment-level cruising remains after
the step because blocks busier than the zone average stay over the line; that is a property
of one rate per zone, not a bug. Capacity cancels in the formula, so the code passes
occupancy shares (`capacity=1.0`).

## Priced hours: one mask from `config/zones.yaml`

`phillyparking.pricing.priced_hours` is the only place that decides when a meter is on (per
zone, weekday / Saturday / Sunday windows; a zone absent from `zones.yaml`, such as the
fixture's `other`, is never priced). `apply_elasticity` (response), `annual_revenue` and
`revenue_curve` (revenue), `hourly_cruising_dwl` (DWL scorer), the Arnott-Inci peak and the
Seattle band statistics all read it. `apply_elasticity` and the DWL functions default to
`priced_hours_only=True` and need `zone_id` on the panel; only the Chatman-Manville sweep
passes `False`, because its design prices every hour.

Revenue per zone = zone capacity x the zone's own annual priced hours x capacity-weighted
priced-hour occupancy (x price); for a full weekly panel that equals 52 x the sum over
priced rows of occupancy x capacity x price. Full compliance is assumed.

The DWL scorer's threshold is the scenario's `pricing_policy.target_vacancy`, falling back to
`pricing_rules.yaml` `arnott_inci.target_vacancy`, so pricer and scorer use the same line.

## Seattle rule and convergence

`pricing/seattle_rule.py` follows SDOT's 2024 rule: one statistic, the band's average
capacity-weighted zone occupancy over the zone's priced hours, per `time_of_day_bands`
(`scenarios.yaml`); above `occ_high` +$0.50, below `occ_low` -$0.50, inside the band hold.
Prices are per zone x band; `priced_hours.hourly_prices` expands them to hours.

The runner reviews annually until no rate moves by more than $0.001 (a fixed point) or prices
repeat with period 2; `rolls_forward_years` is a cap, not a horizon. A two-cycle is reported
as both phases (`diagnostics["phases"]`, and `<name>_phase_a` / `_phase_b` columns in the
comparison table), never as one year's state. With the dead band, a cycle can still occur at
low rates where one $0.50 step changes the price by a larger ratio than 0.85/0.70.

## Chatman-Manville sweep: one rule module, paired runs

`pricing/targeting_rules.py` is the only implementation of the sweep's rules
(`min_vacancy_peak`, `avg_occupancy`, `avg_priced_hours`, `avg_priced_bands`, `seattle_rule`);
`scenarios/cm_sweep.py` runs them and builds the summaries. All rules step +-$0.50 against the same
0.70 / 0.85 band (`chatman_manville:` in `pricing_rules.yaml`, kept equal to Seattle's by a test)
and differ only in the statistic and window. The four zone-level rules honour `zones.yaml` floors
and ceilings; priced-hours rules hold an unpriced zone. Noise is `sigma * z` with `z` fixed per
replication, so every rule, elasticity and sigma sees the same draw (paired). The sweep prices
all 168 hours, so every DWL and revenue metric has a `_total` and a `_priced_hours` form.

Report per cell, never pooled: `cell_table` (median and range), `paired_table` (each rule over the
peak rule, per replication), `window_dependence` (across cells). `ordering_violations` is the
identity that the 168-hour mean rule never beats the peak rule (mean <= q95); it holds by
construction on a diurnal panel. The sweep is a mechanism illustration on a fixture: a real test
would need a DGP with within-zone dispersion and an off-peak price response. Provenance is in
`outputs/tables/cm_sweep_n200.meta.json`. Regenerate with
`scripts/07_export_report_assets.py --rebuild-cm-sweep` (about half an hour).

## `hourly_cruising_dwl` drop-before-merge

`welfare/cruising_dwl.hourly_cruising_dwl` merges `segments[["segment_id", "capacity"]]`
onto the panel. If the panel already carries `capacity` (e.g., from an earlier merge in
`chatman_manville_sweep`), the merge produces `capacity_x`/`capacity_y` and
`df["capacity"]` raises `KeyError`. Always drop conflicting columns first:

```python
df = occupancy_panel.drop(
    columns=[c for c in ("capacity", "zone_id") if c in occupancy_panel.columns]
).merge(segments[["segment_id", "capacity"]], on="segment_id", how="left")
```

## Gini coefficient

`welfare/incidence.py` computes Gini via Lorenz curve. The curve **must** prepend (0, 0):

```python
lorenz = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(sorted_weights) / total_wv])
```

Without the prepended zero, equal-income groups return 0.0625 instead of 0.0.

## Data acquisition: public layers

Every loader goes through `io/raw_cache.load_layer`. A real fetch is cached under the layer's
own filename with a sidecar `<file>.provenance.json` (layer, kind real/synthetic, source, URL,
UTC fetch time); failures are retried, then raise `LayerFetchError`. A synthetic stand-in is
written only with `allow_synthetic=True` / `PHILLYPARKING_ALLOW_SYNTHETIC=1` (scripts:
`--allow-synthetic`), and only as `<stem>.synthetic<suffix>`. A file with no marker is refused
(`UnknownProvenanceError`): the pre-marker loaders saved synthetic layers under real names, so
the old `data/raw/` cache must be refetched with `scripts/01_download_public_data.py --refresh`.
Script 01 exits 1 if any layer is not real; script 02 records every layer's marker in
`data/processed/segments.meta.json` and never skips a join.

Sources (checked live 2026-10-15): Street Centerline (ArcGIS FeatureServer, paged), RPP blocks
(Carto `residential_parking_permit_blocks`, POINTS with `district_number`), neighborhoods
(odp-data-storage GeoJSON), zoning (Carto `zoning_basedistricts`), ACS 5-year block groups
(Census Data API, needs `CENSUS_API_KEY`; geometry and `ALAND` from `cb_<year>_42_bg_500k`;
cenpy cannot request these vintages), LODES8 WAC/RAC plus `pa_xwalk.csv.gz` block internal
points, OSM via osmnx, SEPTA GTFS (one archive holding `google_bus.zip` and `google_rail.zip`,
read as two feeds). There is no public meter inventory: `fetch_meters` raises; meters come from
the Parking Authority release. Census API errors are re-raised without the request URL, which
carries the key.

**Which segments a zone prices** (`zones.yaml` `priced_curb`, `spatial/segments.py`).
`build_segments` drops City centerline classes with no curb (`no_curb_classes`: expressways,
ramps, driveways, walkways; codes from the centerline metadata) and keeps `street_class`.
`assign_priced_curb` sets `zone_box` (the box the segment lies in) and makes `zone_id` that box
only for priced curb, else `other`. With `--meters` priced curb is `has_meter`; without an
inventory it is a stand-in: class 2-5 and `commercial_frontage_share >= min_commercial_share`
(`join_zoning`: share of the zoned land within 25 m, flat ends, in the Commercial/Commercial
Mixed-Use group; zoning stops at the property line, so the buffer must reach across half the
street). The rule is recorded as `priced_curb_rule` in `segments.meta.json`. Changing the rule
or the inventory changes the `philadelphia` anchors: rerun `derive_baseline_anchors.py`.

Join columns are defined in the `spatial/join.py` docstring. `jobs_within_200m` counts LODES
jobs of the blocks whose internal point lies in the buffer; ACS attributes are buffer-share
weighted and renormalised over the covered part. Zone boxes overlap; `zones.zone_for_point`
gives a point to the smallest box containing it. The segment builder keys ids on `seg_id`, or
`osmid|u|v|key`, splits MultiLineStrings, drops reverse twins of two-way OSM edges, and counts
everything in `segments.attrs["build_report"]`.

## Parking Authority data: ingestion and identification

The RTKL packet is at `docs/rtkl/` (`docs/rtkl/README.md`; PPA's RTKL email
`OpenRecordsOfficer@philapark.org`). `ingest/ppa.py` (script `03b_ingest_ppa.py`) reads
sessions, the meter inventory and the rate schedule through `config/ppa_ingest.yaml`, a
column mapping: when the real export arrives, edit the mapping, not the code. It writes a
panel by segment x calendar period x hour of week with `price_usd_per_hr` = the posted rate
in that period; periods are cut at the schedule's effective dates. Occupancy = paid minutes /
(spaces x 60 x calendar dates of that weekday in the period) / (1 - `unpaid_share[zone]`);
`unpaid_use.unpaid_share` must be stated in the config (0.0 = paid occupancy, a lower bound).
Every ambiguity raises `PPAIngestError` (see the module docstring). `runner_inputs` picks one
period for the runner and refuses a period whose posted rates differ from `zones.yaml`.
`--stub` ingests `ppa_stub.make_known_answer_export` into `data/processed/ppa_stub/`, never into
`data/processed/ppa/`.

`elasticity/identification.check_identification` rank-tests the design (zone intercepts,
zone x log price, hour-of-week and period effects) and raises `IdentificationError` when no
zone's elasticity is identified; `fit_elasticity_model` calls it before importing PyMC and
adds `delta[period]` when the panel has a `period` column. Script 04 fits only priced hours of
an ingested panel and exits 1 when nothing is identified (`--allow-prior-only` writes the prior
labelled `prior_only`). One period (one price per zone) identifies nothing; two zones whose
rates both changed, with no unchanged zone, are not identified once the period term is in.

## Allocation, consumer surplus, ranges

`revenue/allocation.py` follows 75 Pa.C.S. § 6109(g) (Act 84 of 2012; citation, text and
retrieval date in `config/allocation.yaml`): General Fund first, up to the $35M minimum, then
the PBD share from what is left, then the School District. The statute has no PBD payee, and
the City's amount is not reduced by expenses, so a PBD can only be paid from the School
District's excess; the unpaid part of its share is `pbd_unfunded_usd`. At the fixture's scale
(a few $M against a $35M minimum) the General Fund takes everything, so PBD and School District
rows are $0 and the PBD's claim shows as unfunded: read the allocation as an order of payment,
not a forecast. The model uses gross meter revenue and no escalator, the statute net revenue
citywide and an escalated minimum.

`cs_change_total` is annual dollars: per zone, the mean nested-logit logsum change per trip
(`runner._zone_trip_cs_change`, vectorised in `welfare/consumer_surplus.py`) times that zone's
status-quo paid space-hours (`annual_revenue(...)["paid_hours"]`), first order, no search-time
term. A zone with paid hours and no price raises; no default price is invented.

Script 05 also writes `scenario_ranges.csv` and `scenario_runs.csv` (copied to `outputs/tables/`
by 06): the other two named elasticities at the headline seed, and the central elasticity at
fixture seeds 0..n-1 (`--n-seeds`, default 20). On segments that are not the synthetic fixture
the occupancy panel does not depend on the seed, so the seed range moves only the CS trips.
`pessimistic` (-0.7) gives the highest revenue because both reforms are net price cuts. A Seattle
two-cycle pools both phases in each range cell. `incidence_by_decile.png` is a constant
placeholder, stamped as such in the figure; `Report.tex` still includes it.
