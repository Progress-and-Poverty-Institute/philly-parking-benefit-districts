### 2-1  The project carries two incompatible cruising-cost models; on the saved Seattle panel the sweep's shape charges $108M a year against the scenarios' $4.0M, and the sign of any net reading of the scenario table depends on which is used
- Severity: **Material** (Blocking for any sentence that reads surplus - revenue loss - cruising as a net; the project states none, see below). Confidence: Verified ran.
- Plan: H1, H2, H3, O3. Prior findings: 15 (surplus row beside annual millions) closed as arithmetic, reopened as meaning.
- Where it enters: `welfare/cruising_dwl.py:25-31` (linear: excess over 0.85 x 5 min x $20) scores the scenarios; `welfare/targeting_welfare.py:65-93` (o^K, cap 0.99, arrivals = served / 1.5 h) scores the sweep. Both read `config/pricing_rules.yaml`. The runner docstring (runner.py:259-260) and CLAUDE.md say the surplus row "is not a welfare total"; `viz/tables.py:34-44` prints surplus, revenue and "Annual cruising DWL" as adjacent rows of the paper's table with no net row, and `comparison_table` likewise.
- What the probe shows (`probe_f_runner_welfare.out`, saved panels, 221,556 priced block-hours a week, 22,653 spaces):
  - Seattle: dCS +$87.9M = transfer $77.3M (existing parkers pay less) + triangle $10.6M; dRevenue -$64.9M. The surplus gain is 88% a transfer from the Authority to incumbents, so dCS + dRev = +$23.0M is the triangle plus the induced parkers' surplus.
  - cruising, status quo -> Seattle: proxy $0.20M -> $4.03M (+$3.8M); the sweep's shape $4.24M -> $107.8M (+$103.6M); at cap 0.95 $30.3M (+$26.0M); with arrivals = latent demand $112.6M.

Under the sweep's shape each arrival at a full 10-space block costs 31.7 minutes (65 at K = 5; the real priced segments have median capacity 8), under the proxy 5 minutes x the excess only.
- What the argument assumes (brief item f): for the integral on baseline demand to be the parkers' surplus when induced demand is clipped at capacity, the parkers priced in by the cut must either find a space or pay a search cost that is counted. ### 2-2  The sweep's breakeven is a property of the search-cost shape: the occupancy cap alone moves it 5x, zone-pool search 14x, the project's own scenario proxy 29x; at the configured 5 minutes the peak rule's win in 52 of 54 cells reverses under every alternative shape
- Severity: **Material** (Blocking for the statement "peak targeting wins at 5 minutes in 52/54 cells" or for the dashed 5-minute verdict line in `cm_breakeven_*.png`, if either is a conclusion; the breakeven *as a breakeven* survives). Confidence: Verified ran.
- Plan: H3, O4, I3. Prior finding 4 (orderings are theorems): closed as redesigned; the residual is that the size of the one measured quantity is the shape's.
- Where it enters: `targeting_welfare.py:65-82` (`_full_blocks_passed` = p/(1-p) with p = o^K; `expected_search_minutes` capped at `occupancy_cap`), `pricing_rules.yaml:44-49`. The docstring: "The shape is that; the level is set by `minutes_at_reference`". Nothing tests or varies the shape (plan O4 confirmed: `tests/test_targeting_welfare.py` pins convexity and the reference level only).
- Evidence (`probe_c_sweep_cs.out`; replica matches the tracked parquet to 0 on welfare_change, cs_change, revenue, search_cost, cruising_dwl for all 3,456 rule-years of replications 0-1). Year-8 breakeven minutes, median [min, max] over the 54 cells, mean_zone vs peak_zone (mean_band vs peak_band in parentheses):
  - tracked (o^K, cap 0.99, served arrivals): 1.93 [1.09, 6.02] (1.90 [1.24, 5.37])
  - cap 0.95 instead of 0.99: 10.2 [4.3, 36.0] (10.1 [4.6, 34.7])
  - search over the zone's aggregate occupancy, as Arnott-Inci model the pool (same o^K form, K = 10, capacity-weighted zone-hour mean): 26.4 [1.7, 266] (32.8 [3.5, 264])
  - the scenarios' own linear proxy (H1): 55.9 [24.4, 188] (54.5 [26.6, 178])
  - arrivals = latent demand instead of served: 1.48 [0.86, 2.65] (small, the other way)
- Reading. The breakeven is finite and positive under every shape (the mechanism "a higher-priced rule wins once cruising is costly enough" is robust). Its *value* is not: with cap 0.95 the median cell needs 10 minutes at the reference block-hour, twice the configured 5 and inside Shoup's 3.5-14; under pool search or the proxy it needs more than any study reports. The cap is the most load-bearing single constant in the sweep: at cap 0.99 a full block costs 31.7 min per arrival, at 0.95 5 min, and in the high-dispersion cells 30-58% of the mean rule's block-hours are full (`share_bh_dgt1_mean`).
- Which of the shape's parts is load-bearing for the size versus the existence: existence, none; size, the cap and the independence-of-spaces convexity (both set how much a *full* block costs relative to an 85% one). Geometric search and no balking enter through the same p/(1-p) and are not separable from the cap. "No spatial correlation" is what the pool variant relaxes; it moves the breakeven most because a peak rule's advantage is precisely the tail blocks that pooling averages away.
- Repro: `probe_c_sweep_cs.py 2`; per-cell tables in `probe_c_breakeven_mean_zone_vs_peak_zone.csv`, `..._mean_band_vs_peak_band.csv`.

### 2-3  Forty percent of the sweep's surplus difference (median; 86% at start occupancy 0.9) is credited to block-hours that were over-full before any rule acted; scored on served demand the breakeven range is 0.85-2.8 minutes, not 1.1-6.0, and most of the dispersion dependence disappears
- Severity: **Material**. Confidence: Verified ran.
- Plan: H2, H3, I3; brief item (c). Bears on the plan's "3 to 7 minutes at high block dispersion".
- Where it enters: `cm_sweep.py:195` (`cs_change_usd(d0, ...)` on latent d0, unclipped) against `cm_sweep.py:188-189` and `targeting_welfare.py:91` (search cost on served arrivals o x K / 1.5, o capped at 1). The mean rule, which prices lower, keeps those block-hours over-full and is credited the full latent surplus on them.
