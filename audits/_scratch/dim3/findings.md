- Severity: **Material** (Blocking for the sentence in plan section 1, "tell us the structure of the trade-offs, not their size in Philadelphia", if it is carried into a deliverable as the model's claim). Confidence: Verified ran (probe_a; dim2 probe_a / probe_b / probe_f; dim4b p06_cs, p03_stat_sensitivity).
- Plan: O1, O2, O3, D1, C1b, F1; prior finding 1 (open, as dim2 says, with the cause moved to the kernels) and 3.
- Gap analysis. Shown: on a stated baseline that sits below the band in every zone (S1), two rules cut; under the law a cut lowers revenue, raises surplus and raises a monotone cruising proxy (S3-S5). Concluded: "the structure of the trade-offs". The step: the signs are entailed by "it is a cut" for any downward-sloping demand with abs(e) < 1 and any monotone proxy; "it is a cut" is entailed by the stated baseline (2-5: the outer zones cannot be above the band at any level setting; 2-10: CCC's position is the inventory stand-in's). So the table's sign pattern carries no information beyond its inputs. What a reader could not have derived in advance: (i) which reform cuts more and (ii) CCC's rate. (i) is the floors' (2-4: the ordering reverses with no floors and with the Report's floors) and the elasticity's (4b-15: reverses at -0.2); (ii) is the statistic's ($1.00-2.86, 4b). - Which statements about C1 are which: (i) true by construction of the stated inputs: every direction, the sign of every row, CCC's fixed point sitting at 0.8499; (ii) measured properties of the real segments: the covariate ranking (who is busier), the zone composition, priced capacity by zone (a rule output of a stated rule), priced hours; (iii) extrapolations: every dollar level, the reform ordering, the CCC rate, the allocation rows. ### 3-2  "That breakeven is the result" (CLAUDE.md:180-181) presents as the sweep's finding a quantity the design does not identify: c* > 0 is a theorem, and its value is the scorer's shape and scoring rule, the demand form's extrapolation and, in a third of the cells, the configured bounds
- Severity: **Material**. Confidence: Verified ran (probe_d, probe_d2, probe_b; dim2 probe_c; dim4b p08).
- Plan: H3, I2, I3, O4, O6; prior finding 4 (closed as redesign; this is the residual), 13; dim2 2-2, 2-3, 2-7; dim4b 4b-2.
- Theorem versus measurement. In every same-instrument cell the mean rule prices lower (mean <= q95 of the same block-hours), so it has more surplus + revenue (total surplus falls in price at zero marginal cost) and more search cost (the shape is increasing in occupancy): c* = gain / search-difference > 0 is a consequence of the definitions, which is why dim1 found the same sign pattern in all 108 cells and why test_welfare_ordering_is_not_an_identity can never fail (3-6). The only thing measured is the size of c*.
- What sets the size. (a) The shape: cap 0.95 x5, pool search x14, the project's own proxy x29 (2-2). (b) The scoring rule: 40% (median) to 86% of the surplus difference is credited to block-hours over-full before any rule acted (2-3). (c) The form: the peak rules spend 88% of their e = -0.7 rep-years beyond the law's revenue peak, facing elasticity up to 1.85 (4b-2, p08_o6_prices.out); constant elasticity moves c* by up to 0.7 min at year 3. (d) The bounds: probe_d_bounds_in_sweep.out: in 9 of 54 cells the mean rule's year-8 price equals the all-floor mean price (every zone at its zones.yaml floor; probe_d2: $1.00 in all five zones at m = 0.6, e = -0.4, tau 0 and 0.3), and in 17 cells the peak rule sits on a 5.29 plateau that probe_d2 resolves as fishtown and south_philly at their $4.00 ceilings and ucity at its $6.00 ceiling (cca and ccc at $6.00, interior). These are Philadelphia's bounds applied to a fixture the project says is "nothing like Philadelphia's". In those cells c* is partly the bound's: the mean rule cannot cut further and the peak rule cannot raise further, so the gap between them, and hence c*, is capped by config rather than by the statistics.
- Is the comparison fair? Same band, step, start price, law and bounds: yes, as rules of the game. But the bounds bind one side in 9 cells and the other in 17; the form's extrapolation enters the higher-priced side more (S24); and the noise axis biases only the peak statistic (3-4). None of this is a bug; all of it is why c* is the scorer's and the config's, not the rules'.
- The honest statement: "under this search-cost shape, this scoring of surplus, this demand form and these bounds, the two rules tie at about 1-3 minutes (blocks alike) to 3-7 minutes (dispersed) at 95% on a 10-space block; under the project's other cruising model the tie is at 24-188 minutes." CLAUDE.md's sentence ("That breakeven is the result: below it the lower-priced rule gives more welfare, above it the higher-priced one") is true as the definition of c* and false as a claim that the sweep found where the tie lies. README's paragraph ("the paired tables report each comparison's breakeven") is a description, not a verdict, and is clean.
