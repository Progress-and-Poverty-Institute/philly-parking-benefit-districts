## Scope

**The argument under review.** Three claims rest on one pipeline. (1) *White paper* (PPI /
5th Square, `outputs/reports/white_paper/Report.tex`): Philadelphia under-prices its curb; a
Seattle-style ±$0.50 rule or an Arnott–Inci "price that eliminates cruising at the busiest
hour" would yield about $36M (Seattle) or $25M to the General Fund plus $11M to parking
benefit districts (full PBD) against a $43M status quo, and would reduce cruising. (2) *Academic
paper* (`notebooks/paper/*.qmd`, README): targeting minimum vacancy at the peak gives about 53×
lower cruising deadweight loss and 2.5× higher revenue than targeting average occupancy,
"confirming" Chatman and Manville (2014). (3) *Operational tool*: the same engine runs on real
PPA transactions by a schema-stable swap once the Right-to-Know request closes. The evidence for
(1) and (2) is the output of `scripts/05_run_scenarios.py` and `chatman_manville_sweep`. The
inferential step is that a synthetic occupancy panel, calibrated to a stated peak-occupancy
target and pushed through constant-elasticity demand and two pricing rules, tells us the
direction and rough magnitude of the real policies.

## Verdict

The pipeline is internally coherent and reproducible, and none of its three claims is supported
by it. Every published number descends from a random synthetic fixture whose zone labels,
capacities and covariates are independent draws; the paper's listed data inputs never reach a
result. Inside that fixture the sign of every reform row is set by three unsourced calibration
constants: a null control with no cruising anywhere reproduces the published pattern (reform
revenue down about a fifth, cruising cost up), and making the paper's premise true inside the
model reverses it. The full-PBD row is decided by which "peak" statistic is used, not by the
policy. The Chatman–Manville sweep's two orderings cannot come out the other way under its
design, so it tests nothing. What the project can honestly claim today is a scenario engine
that maps (panel, rule, elasticity) to (prices, revenue, a cruising proxy), exercised on a toy,
plus one conditional: zones whose weekday mean sits under 70% get cut by the implemented rules
and zones whose lunch peak is over 85% get raised, and which world Philadelphia is in has not
been measured.

### [BLOCKING] 1. The reform signs are a calibration input, and the white paper's conclusions contradict its own table

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter R1 (both halves survive); dim2 F1, dim3 F1–F2, dim1 D1-2/D1-3.
**What's wrong:** `demand/synthetic_baseline.py` sets every segment's occupancy as a linear
interpolation between `offpeak_floor = 0.20` and a peak that runs from `low_demand_peak = 0.62`
to `peak_target = 0.92` by demand rank. None of the three constants has a source (the docstring
says "anchored to literature" and names none; the Report's "calibrated to published PPA
occupancy statistics" cites nothing; `research/*.md` and `docs/external_sources.md` contain no
Philadelphia occupancy statistic). On the fixture this puts Center City Core's weekday-lunch
zone-hour q90 at 0.855 (just over the Seattle trigger) and its pooled all-hours q90 at 0.807
(under the Arnott trigger), and every other zone's peak at 0.62–0.68. So the status quo has
almost no modelled cruising (DWL $6,005; 1.3% of segment-hours above 0.85·capacity), the Seattle
rule cuts four of five zones and the Arnott rule cuts five of five, revenue falls 16–17% and the
cruising proxy rises 12–16×. The executive summary (Report.tex ~135–145) nevertheless says
demand-responsive pricing "could generate approximately $36–43 million" (the top of that range
is the do-nothing figure) and "can substantially reduce cruising"; the only prose that notices
the direction is the table note explaining the CCC cut.
**Evidence:** Null control (`peak_target` 0.849, so no segment-hour anywhere is above 0.85 and
status-quo DWL is exactly $0): Seattle still loses 21.4% of revenue and creates $79,605 of DWL;
full PBD loses 24.3% and creates $69,868. Recovery (`peak_target` 0.99, `low_demand_peak` 0.93,
`offpeak_floor` 0.40, every zone over-full at peak): Seattle +16.0% revenue, DWL −67.7%; full
PBD +13.7%, −78.3%. Across an 11-setting calibration sweep the Seattle signs flip only once every
outer zone is over-full (`low_demand_peak` ≥ ~0.90); the Arnott revenue sign flips at ~0.88. In
the tracked run 70% of the Seattle revenue decline ($492K of $703K) and 99% of its DWL rise come
from cutting CCA from $3.50 to $1.50, and CCA's demand is drawn from the same distribution as
Fishtown's (`spatial/synthetic.py`: jobs 50–3,000 for every non-CCC segment), so the plausible
intermediate world (core zones over-full, corridors not) cannot even be expressed by the three
constants. Git shows the earlier Markdown draft said the Seattle figure was "slightly lower than
status quo"; the LaTeX conversion dropped the clause and replaced "$40–50M compared to the
roughly $43M currently collected" with "$36–43M".
**Refuter corrections kept:** full-PBD DWL stays below Seattle DWL in all 11 calibrations, so
the design does rank the two reforms on the proxy; "no rank" overstates. The $10–12M PBD figure
is consistent with the computed $1.07M × 10. The paper's "curb subsidy" number is a hand
calculation about residential permits in its section 1.1, outside the pipeline.
**Consequence if unfixed:** the white paper asserts a revenue gain and a cruising reduction
that its own model denies, on the strength of three constants nobody can source.
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/R1/probe.py` (and
`sweep.py`); `audits/_scratch/dim3/probe_null.py`; `cat outputs/tables/comparison_table.csv`.
**Cost to fix:** no code. The scenario section must become a conditional on measured peak
occupancy by zone and hour, or wait for the RTKL data; the executive summary must follow the
table's signs.

### [BLOCKING] 3. The full-PBD row is a property of the chosen peak statistic, not of the policy

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter R2 (survives, scoped to the full-PBD column and every comparison using it); dim2 F2, dim3 F3, dim1 D1-7.
**What's wrong:** The paper describes the rule as the price that "eliminates cruising at the
busiest hour". `scenarios/runner.py` `_arnott_zone_prices` computes
p* = p0 · (0.85 / q90)^(1/ε) where q90 is the 90th percentile of occupancy over **all
segment-hours in the zone across all 168 hours**, a pool that is 57–64% unpriced overnight and
Sunday hours; the pooled q90 sits at only the 77th–85th percentile of the priced-hour
distribution, and in CCA and South Philly a third to a half of the "peak" it prices to happens
overnight. Holding that quantile at 0.85 leaves exactly 10% of each zone's segment-hours at or
above the 0.85·capacity line that `welfare/cruising_dwl.py` integrates over (observed
10.05–10.66% per zone after the step), so the scenario's DWL is positive by construction. The
10-year loop is a one-step fixed point. `CLAUDE.md` records that q90 replaced the all-hours mean
because the mean "can push the computed optimal price to the floor"; no reason for q90 over any
other peak statistic is recorded and no sensitivity was run.
**Evidence:** one-step outcomes at ε = −0.4 (R2 `variant_table.csv`): as coded (pooled q90, 168 h)
CCC $3.51, revenue −17.4%, DWL $73,578; q90 of priced segment-hours CCC $4.05, −10.5%, $25,754;
busiest capacity-weighted priced zone-hour CCC $4.06, −21.2%, $188,991; busiest priced
segment-hour CCC $4.88, +2.9%, DWL $0. Quantile level alone (q80–q99, pooled) spans −29% to −1%
and $252K to $2K. Under the paper's own wording ("busiest hour") CCC is above target and its
rate rises, so the table note's explanation for the CCC cut describes an artifact of pooling
unpriced hours; and whether full PBD beats Seattle on the proxy ($97,160) depends on the
statistic. The same repo uses q95 as "peak" in the sweep and q90 of weekday 11–14 zone-hours in
the Seattle rule.
**Refuter corrections kept:** zone-mean capacity cancels in the formula (immaterial); DWL
independence from ε breaks when a floor binds (ε = −0.2 gives $69,793); the revenue sign versus
the status quo flips only under the busiest-segment-hour reading, while the natural readings
flip the CCC direction and the Seattle-versus-PBD DWL ranking; "selected for its output"
overstates the record, which shows an undocumented choice with no sensitivity check.
**Consequence if unfixed:** "$25 million to the general fund plus $11 million to PBDs", the CCC
rate cut and the full-PBD cruising figure report a pooling choice, and the label
"welfare-maximising" is supportable under none of the variants (nothing in the pipeline computes
a welfare maximum; the proxy has no vacancy cost).
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/R2/probe.py`;
`audits/_scratch/dim3/probe_arnott.py`.
**Cost to fix:** hours. Choose and justify one statistic over priced hours at the zone-hour
level, make the DWL scorer read the scenario's `target_vacancy`, report the one-step table above
as a sensitivity, and let the prose follow the resulting sign.

### [BLOCKING] 4. The Chatman–Manville sweep's orderings are theorems; "empirical test" and "confirms the hypothesis" do not follow

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter R3 (sub-claim 1 survives "Major"; sub-claims 2 and 3 Material, filed as finding 13); dim2 F4, dim3 F4, dim1 D1-10.
**What's wrong:** `scenarios/compare.py` `chatman_manville_sweep` applies the same thresholds
(cut $0.50 below 0.70, raise $0.50 above 0.85) to two statistics of the same zone panel: the
mean over all 168 hours × segments ("avg_occupancy") and the 95th percentile
("min_vacancy_peak"), from a flat $2.00 start, with DWL scored against the same 0.85 the peak
rule targets. On any panel with a diurnal shape the mean is below the q95, so the avg rule's
adjustment is never above the peak rule's, prices stay ordered every year, occupancy is ordered
element-wise, and DWL and (for |ε| < 1) revenue follow. With this calibration the 168-hour zone
means are 0.43–0.50 and the priced-hour means 0.47–0.71, so any averaging comparator cuts toward
the $0.50 floor while the peak rule mostly holds. README line 8 calls the paper an "empirical
test", line 91 says the sweep "confirms the paper's core hypothesis", Report.tex 352–358 calls
the finding "stark" and 563–564 says it "can be re-run on real data to validate" it; the Council
cover letter promises a paper "testing whether" peak targeting outperforms. `docs/PLAN.md` line
93 states the sweep's purpose as mapping "the regime where" peak targeting outperforms; no such
regime boundary exists.
**Evidence:** paired runs on the config grid (6 ε × 3 σ, 5 replications, 5 years): 0 violations
of p_avg ≤ p_peak and rev_avg ≤ rev_peak in 2,700 zone-years; DWL ordering holds in 450 of 450
replication-years at the total level (32 zone-year exceptions arise from the 1.0 clip at
|ε| ≥ 0.5). Reversal attempts: a 12-point calibration grid of `baseline_occupancy`, the other
panel generator, n = 120, ε = −1.5, horizons 1–10, σ = 0, two "realistic" mean > q95
constructions: none reverses (year-5 ratios 16×–118×); only a knife-edge two-point panel or
ε > 0 does. Fair comparators, paired, year 5: avg over priced hours 30.5× / 1.66×; SFpark-style
three bands 33.2× / 1.69×; Seattle peak-window mean 17.6× / 1.36×; **the project's own Seattle
rule** (`pricing/seattle_rule.py`, raise on a peak-window q90, cut on the priced-hours mean)
1.2× / 0.97×, beating the peak rule on DWL in 6 of 18 cells and on revenue in 16 of 18. The
research note's own section 3.7 defines the SFpark/Seattle averaging rule per time band; the
sweep's comparator is weaker than the rule Chatman and Manville criticised. Notebook 08's
hand-written discussion ("under highly elastic demand the two rules converge") is contradicted
by the sweep (ratio rises from 32× at −0.2 to 128× at −0.7).
**Consequence if unfixed:** the academic paper's central result is a restatement of its
definitions. Real data cannot change the ordering, only its size, so the "validate on real
data" promise is empty.
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/R3/q2_paired_instrumented.py`
then `q4_fair_comparators.py`; `audits/_scratch/dim3/probe_cm.py`.
**Cost to fix:** a redesign of the experiment: a DGP with within-zone dispersion and off-peak
price response, a comparator defined on priced hours by band (as the research note defines it),
and the project's Seattle rule as the policy-relevant benchmark.

### [MATERIAL] 5. Zone elasticities are unidentified by design, and the academic draft claims a fit that does not exist

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter R4 (downgraded from Blocking: Material for the draft, Advisory for the tool); dim2 F5, dim3 F7, dim4b 11.
**What's wrong:** `elasticity/hierarchical_bayes.py` models log occupancy = α_z + ε_z·log p +

rank 5 of 10 and the posterior for ε_z returns the prior (posterior sd / prior sd 0.90–0.96,

runner, script 06 and the app read the YAML scalar −0.40. On the shipped artifacts script 04
would not even reach its `prior_only` fallback: the fixture panel's ids do not intersect
`segments.parquet`, 0 of 20,160 rows survive the price filter, and the project's fit returns an
empty summary labelled `pymc_posterior`. `01-introduction.qmd` line 15 lists "a Bayesian
estimate of zone-level elasticities" as a contribution, `02-methods.qmd` line 23 says "We fit a
hierarchical Bayesian model with zone-level random slopes on log-price using PyMC", `index.qmd`
line 8 says scenarios were "rolled forward under" it. `docs/rtkl/README.md` 105–107 says one
zone-month of transactions suffices for a non-degenerate elasticity posterior; one zone-month
has one price.
**Refuter corrections kept:** `tests/test_hierarchical_bayes.py` is a correct parameter-recovery
test and must vary price within zone; the gap is a missing pipeline-level test. The RTKL window
already straddles the 2025-07-01 rate change (CCC $3→$4, CCA $2.50→$3.50), which would identify

**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/R4/q_probe_analytic.py`;

## Refuted candidates

- **The baseline is price-independent, so scenarios apply elasticity to a baseline that embeds
  no price response (plan D7).** Refuted (R10 f2): the elasticity step *is* the intended price
  response; a baseline conditioned on covariates with the price effect applied afterwards is a
  coherent design. What remains true is that zone price differences cannot appear as baseline
  occupancy differences, which matters only for identification (finding 5).
- **The fixture's zone labels disagree with its geometry 76% of the time (plan B7).** Refuted
  (R10 d): the labels are the design; nothing re-derives zones from geometry on the result path.
- **`cm_sweep_n200.parquet` has unknown provenance (plan I7).** Refuted (R3, R11b): it reproduces
  row for row from `chatman_manville_sweep(n_replications=200)` with defaults. What survives is
  that no script makes the call and the figures have no producer (finding 21).
- **The Arnott statistic was selected for its output (plan F3 (iv)).** Refuted as stated (R2):
  the commit record gives a conceptual reason for dropping the mean; the replacement was an
  undocumented choice with no sensitivity, which finding 3 records.
- **Zone-mean capacity in the Arnott formula is a defect.** Refuted (R2): capacity cancels.
- **The full-PBD DWL's independence from ε is a defect.** Refuted (R2): any target-hitting rule
  yields the target occupancy regardless of ε; the content is finding 3 (ii).
- **The design yields no rank among the reforms.** Refuted in part (R1): full-PBD DWL is below
  Seattle DWL in all 11 calibrations; the revenue rank does flip in one setting.
- **"mean ≤ q95 is an identity" and "the revenue ratio is literally 4^(1+ε)".** Refuted as
  stated (R3): the ordering premise holds for every panel with a diurnal shape (one knife-edge
  two-point counterexample); the noise-free revenue ratio is 2.05 and clipping and noise lift it
  to 2.50. Finding 4 and 13 carry the corrected statements.
- **`tests/test_hierarchical_bayes.py` is a defective guard because it varies price within
  zone.** Refuted (R4): a parameter-recovery test must; the gap is a missing pipeline-level test
  (finding 20).
- **The incidence figure is an undisclosed placeholder.** Refuted (R8e): the figure and caption
  say "placeholder"; the surviving defect is the abstract's "quantifies the equity case"
  (finding 16).
- **The Seattle rule's $10 ceiling and 0.95 two-step tier distort the result.** Refuted (R7b):
  neither ever binds.
- **Script 04 falls back to `prior_only` on the shipped artifacts.** Refuted (R4): it fits on 0
  rows and labels an empty summary `pymc_posterior`, which is worse (finding 5).
- **"Silently" (scripts 05/06 swap in the fixture with no signal).** Corrected (R5): a WARNING is
  logged; the artifacts carry no marker (finding 2).
- **Candidate O14 "test suite > 5 min" as a finding.** Closed by commit 006e71d, which records
  the measured cost and the slow test; kept only as a cost fact (A11).
- **Constant elasticity is over-stretched (0.33×–2×) and the elasticity config blocks are read
  by no code.** Refuted as stated (R12a): the headline runs apply 0.43×–1.125×, and three of
  the four blocks are read off the result path. The surviving sub-item is the Lehner & Peer
  attribution (finding 26).
- **The $20/h value of time is unsourced.** Refuted (R12c): it is a defensible rounding of the
  CPI-scaled USDOT figure the config cites; the residue is that the config key is dead (A13).
- **The zone bboxes are wrong inputs.** Refuted as an input defect (R12c, R10d): no result
  depends on them; they appear only in a figure (A14).
- **Market-priced permits are promised and not implemented.** Refuted as a Material finding
  (R12d): the Report does not promise them; only config descriptions and the research note do
  (A15).
