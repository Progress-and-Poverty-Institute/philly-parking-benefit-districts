## Scope

**The argument under review.** After the first audit (`audit_2026-10-14.md`, 27 findings) and pull
requests #4 to #12, the project claims three narrower things. (1) *Scenario pipeline*: on 3,145 real
City-centerline segments treated as metered (commercial-frontage stand-in for the missing Parking
Authority inventory; 22,653 spaces), under a stated weekly occupancy profile and a stated exponential
demand law anchored at the posted rates, a Seattle-style per-band rule and an Arnott-Inci
busiest-priced-hour rule both cut Center City rates; revenue falls from $140.7M to $75.7M (Seattle) and
$82.0M (full PBD), consumer surplus rises $87.9M / $80.6M, the cruising proxy rises from $0.2M to
$4.0M / $3.8M; the project says these are conditional on the stated levels and tell "the structure of
the trade-offs, not their size in Philadelphia". (2) *Chatman-Manville sweep*: on an n=120 synthetic
fixture, targeting the peak statistic beats targeting the mean on welfare (surplus + revenue - search
cost) only when search at 95% occupancy on a 10-space block costs more than a breakeven of about 1 to
3 minutes when blocks are alike and 3 to 7 at high block dispersion. (3) *Real-data path*: every raw
layer is real and provenance-marked; the Parking Authority release can be ingested into a segment x
period x hour panel; a rank test decides identification before PyMC runs. The inferential step
behind (1) and (2) is that stated inputs pushed through the rules reveal structure even where they
cannot give size.

## Verdict

The pipeline now runs on real streets, reproduces every tracked number bit for bit, and states its
levels, its demand law and its search-cost shape as assumptions; the first audit's four Blocking
findings are closed as defects. Its two deliverables are still not findings. The scenario table's
signs are entailed by the stated inputs (for four of the five zones by the hand-drawn kernels and the
mix, not even by the levels; for Center City by which blocks the stand-in meters), its dollar sizes
are mostly the distance from the posted rate to an unsourced floor, and the between-reform
differences are one zone's rate under one statistic, so "the structure of the trade-offs" is a
restatement of the configuration. The sweep's breakeven is positive by construction and its size is
the occupancy cap's: quoted at a 95% reference block-hour that does not drive it, it is 1-7 minutes;
re-expressed where the cost is incurred it is 10-14 minutes per arrival at a full block, and the
configured level sits above the range the config cites. What the project can honestly claim is a
reproducible scenario engine on real segments whose outputs are conditional on constants it can now
name, a welfare scorer whose mechanism (a higher-priced rule wins once cruising is costly enough) is
robust and whose number is not, and a real-data path that will replace the levels on covered segments
but cannot touch the shape that sets every welfare magnitude.

### [BLOCKING] 1. The scenario table's signs are entailed by the stated inputs and its sizes by the unsourced floors, so "the structure of the trade-offs, not their size" is not something this pipeline delivers

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter RA (A1 narrowed, A1(ii) survives, A3 narrowed to Advisory);
dim2 2-4, 2-5, 2-10; dim3 3-1 (S1-S9); dim4b 4b-1, 4b-16; dim4a 4a-9; dim1 1-3, 1-6.
**What's wrong:** The claim under review (plan section 1; the memory file; `CLAUDE.md`'s hedge
"conditional on the levels") is that a stated occupancy profile pushed through the two rules shows
the structure of the trade-offs. On the real panel every zone's busiest capacity-weighted priced
zone-hour is below the Arnott target (ccc 0.758, ucity 0.641, cca 0.621, fishtown 0.593,
south_philly 0.538) and every Seattle band mean below 0.70 (ccc 0.600 priced-hour mean), so both
rules cut everywhere; under any downward-sloping demand with abs(e) < 1 a cut lowers revenue, raises
surplus and raises a monotone cruising proxy. Every sign in `comparison_table.csv` is therefore a
theorem of "it is a cut", and "it is a cut" is a theorem of the stated inputs. For the four outer
zones it is not even the three level constants' doing: at the stated kernel mix (`mix_temperature`
2.5, mix weights 1.0 / 1.2 / 0.9; `demand/synthetic_baseline.py:43-112, 161-174`), no level setting
up to peak 0.99 / low-demand peak 0.85 / off-peak floor 0.60 lifts cca, fishtown or south_philly
above 0.85 at the busiest hour (ucity reaches 0.8502 at the extreme corner), because 34-55% of each
outer zone's capacity is residential-leaning and peaks at 8:00 while the rest peaks at 13:00, so the
zone aggregate averages segments at different points of their own profiles; the Seattle band means
never leave the cut region at any setting. Only at `mix_temperature` 0, or with the residential
kernel removed, do outer zones cross. CCC's direction is set by which blocks the stand-in meters:
metering only the busier half of CCC's commercial curb gives 0.887 (Arnott raises). Of what the
table reports beyond the signs, the sizes are the floors' (finding 4: four zones end at identical
floor prices under both reforms, carrying 68-75% of the revenue change, 66-69% of the DWL change and
67-73% of the surplus), and the between-reform differences are CCC alone, where the rate spans
$1.00 (mean statistic) to $2.86 (max) across peak statistics. Nothing a reader could not derive
from `config/zones.yaml` and `config/demand_baseline.yaml` remains.
**What survives as a correct description:** "on a stated baseline that sits below the band in every
zone, a dead-band rule and a target-vacancy pricer both cut, with the ordinary consequences of a cut;
the one quantity the rules decide is CCC's rate, which depends on the peak statistic; the rest is the
distance from the posted rate to a stated floor, through the law." The covariate ranking (who is
busier) is measured; no level or direction is. The pilot evidence the project holds and never cites
(research note: SFpark's rates fell 4% on average; DDOT found 9-10% of blocks at 85%) is consistent
with the cut's sign and inconsistent with its size (35-62% of revenue, finding 4).
**Refuter corrections kept:** the quantifier is conditional on the stated mix constants, not
absolute; at the default levels the intensity anchors bind before the mix does (the aligned-peak upper
bound is already 0.71-0.77); the residential share is 34-55%, not 43-55%; the negative unclipped
Arnott prices are the exact solution of an unattainable target under the stated law, clipped at
`runner.py:193`, not a code defect; `CLAUDE.md` attributes the sign dependence to the levels only.
**Consequence if unfixed:** the paper rewrite would present as the model's structural finding a
restatement of its configuration, with the hedge pointing at the wrong constants (the levels, when
the kernels, the mix, the floors and the stand-in decide).
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/dim2/probe_b_levels.py` and
`probe_b2_offpeak.py`; `audits/_scratch/refute/RA/ra_mix.py`; `audits/_scratch/dim3/probe_a_dwl_by_zone.py`;
`audits/_scratch/dim4b/p03_stat_sensitivity.py`; `cat outputs/tables/comparison_table.csv`.
**Cost to fix:** no code for the honest statement (a day of prose in the rewrite). To make the
scenarios say something about Philadelphia: observed occupancy by zone and hour (Request B), and a
baseline whose within-day shape is fitted rather than drawn (the kernels cannot express a corridor
zone with a common peak).

### [BLOCKING] 2. The Chatman-Manville sweep's breakeven is positive by construction and its size is the occupancy cap's, so "that breakeven is the result" and the 1 to 7 minutes quoted for it are not findings about the rules

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter RB (B1(i) survives narrowed, B1(ii) survives, B1(iv) and
(v) narrowed to Advisory, B2 narrowed to Advisory, B3 refuted); dim3 3-2, 3-12, S14-S20; dim2 2-2;
dim1 1-4; dim4b 4b-2, 4b-4; prior finding 4 (residual).
**What's wrong:** `CLAUDE.md` (Chatman-Manville section) states the sweep's result as "that
breakeven is the result: below it the lower-priced rule gives more welfare, above it the
higher-priced one", and the claim under review quotes it as 1 to 3 minutes at 95% occupancy on a
10-space block when blocks are alike and 3 to 7 at high dispersion. Two things are wrong with that as
a finding. (a) The sign is a theorem. In every same-instrument comparison (`mean_zone` vs
`peak_zone`, `mean_band` vs `peak_band`) the mean rule prices at or below the peak rule (mean <= q95
of the same block-hours), total surplus falls in price at zero marginal cost, and search cost rises
in occupancy, so surplus + revenue is higher and search cost higher for the mean rule in every
replication: verified on all 43,200 rule-years per pair, including the 12 beyond the revenue peak.
c* > 0 therefore follows from the definitions; the sweep's only content is its size. (It is not a
theorem for `seattle_rule` vs `peak_band`: 63 rule-years have c* <= 0.) (b) The size is the
scorer's, not the rules'. 92% (median over cells) of the search-cost gap between the two rules comes
from block-hours at 100% occupancy, where the cost per arrival is set by `occupancy_cap`
(`welfare/targeting_welfare.py:78`; `pricing_rules.yaml:49`): at caps 0.999 / 0.99 / 0.95 the 54-cell
median breakeven is 0.21 / 1.90 / 9.51 minutes, although it is quoted in minutes at the 95%
reference block-hour, which does not drive it. Re-expressed as minutes at a full block, c* is stable
across caps (medians 9.5-13.8, maxima 26-32). The configured level, 5 minutes at 95%, implies 31.7
minutes per arrival at a full block, above the 3.5-14 minute range the config itself cites from
Shoup, and that range is the average cruising time of those who cruise, a different quantity from
the expected search time of all arrivals (A6). At 14 full-block minutes the peak rule gives more
welfare in 39 of 54 cells, at 8 minutes in 1 to 6, at 3.5 in none; the "52 of 54 at the config
level" count rests on the 32-minute cost. Under the project's other cruising model, the scenarios'
linear proxy, the tie is at 24-188 minutes; under search over the zone's aggregate occupancy (the
Arnott-Inci pool) 26-33. The bounds in `zones.yaml` bind in 48 of 54 cells but move c* by under 0.2
minutes except at start occupancy 0.60 (-25%); the demand form moves the median little (1.90 vs 2.06
at 100 replications; the two tracked sweeps differ only in form) but the range top a lot (18.5 ->
7.3), which is why "up to 19 minutes" is a pre-PR-#12 number (commit 55be807) that survives only in
the plan and the memory file (A19).
**What survives as a correct description:** "under this search-cost shape and cap, this scoring of
surplus (finding 5), this demand form and these bounds, the two rules tie at about 10-14 minutes of
search per arrival at a full block (1-3 minutes at the 95% reference when blocks are alike, 3-7 when
dispersed); whether Philadelphia's full-block search time is above or below that is unmeasured, and
the project's own cited range sits below it." The mechanism (a higher-priced rule wins once cruising
is costly enough) is robust to every shape tried; the number is not.
**Consequence if unfixed:** the academic paper's result would be a constant from `pricing_rules.yaml`
read back as a measurement, quoted at a reference level that does not determine it, and its "peak
wins at the configured level" verdict would rest on a full-block search cost twice the top of the
range the project cites.
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/RB/rb3_replica.py` (cap
and served-demand variants; `rb3_replica_default.out`, `rb4_fullblock_units.out`) and `rb1_tracked.py`
(the theorem on the tracked parquet); `audits/_scratch/dim2/probe_c_sweep_cs.py 2`;
`audits/_scratch/dim3/probe_b_tracked_sweep.py`; `cat outputs/tables/cm_sweep_window_dependence.csv`.
**Cost to fix:** report c* in full-block minutes with the cap as an explicit axis (a column and a
rerun of the paired tables, no new sweep); state the sign as a property of the design; replace the
5-minute line in `cm_breakeven_*.png` with the cited range; half a day plus the prose.

### [MATERIAL] 5. The sweep's surplus term counts parkers who never get a space while its search-cost term counts only those who do, which credits the lower-priced rule with surplus on block-hours that were over-full before any rule acted

**Confidence:** Verified (ran). Refuter RB (B1(iii) survives); dim2 2-3; dim3 S17.
**What's wrong:** `scenarios/cm_sweep.py:195` scores consumer surplus with `cs_change_usd` on the
latent demand `d0`, unclipped, while the search cost (`cm_sweep.py:188-189`,
`targeting_welfare.py:91`) counts arrivals as served cars (occupancy capped at 1). The docstring's
rationale (`targeting_welfare.py:19-22`: parkers who cannot find a space "pay the search cost
instead") does not hold at the start state, where only served arrivals are charged. A block-hour at
latent demand 1.5 therefore contributes 1.5x the surplus loss of a full block when its price rises
and the same search cost as a full block, and the mean rule, which prices lower and keeps such
block-hours over-full, is credited with the full latent surplus on them. At start occupancy 0.9 with
block dispersion 0.6, 68% of priced block-hours are over-full before any rule acts; the share of the
surplus difference credited to such block-hours is 40% at the median cell and 86% at start 0.9.
Either consistent treatment (surplus on served demand, or search cost on latent arrivals) lowers the
median breakeven by 15-45% and halves the top of its range (2.6-2.9 against 4.5 minutes at the 95%
reference). The error favours the mean rule, so correcting it strengthens the peak rule's case; it
does not cancel the cap's effect (finding 2), which runs the other way and is larger.
**Consequence if unfixed:** the "3 to 7 minutes at high dispersion" part of the quoted result is
mostly this credit; the dispersion dependence largely disappears when the welfare object is made
consistent.
**Reproduce:** `C:/Users/druss/miniconda3/python.exe audits/_scratch/refute/RB/rb3_replica.py`
(`rb3_replica_default.out`, column `c_cs_served`);
`audits/_scratch/dim2/probe_c_sweep_cs.py 2` (columns `share_bh_dgt1_*`, served-demand breakevens).
**Cost to fix:** one line to score surplus on `min(d, 1)` x capacity, or arrivals on latent demand,
